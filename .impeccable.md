# AYSO Region 13 — Design Context

## Users

Parents of youth soccer players (ages 4–19) in Pasadena, Altadena, La Cañada, and surrounding communities. They have varying levels of soccer knowledge and technical comfort. Most arrive with a specific job to do — sign up a kid, find a field, check a schedule, figure out what to bring on game day — often on a phone, often in a hurry (between games, on the sideline, after work).

Secondary users: volunteers (referees, coaches, team managers, board members) looking for training, forms, and resources; and the small group of board/staff editors who keep content current via Pages CMS.

The site has three jobs, in priority order:
1. **Help people sign up** for soccer programs.
2. **Encourage volunteers** — recruiting and supporting refs, coaches, managers.
3. **Deliver information** — schedules, fields, policies, FAQs.

## Brand Personality

**Welcoming. A little scrappy. Playful.**

This is a 50+ year-old volunteer-run community organization, not a corporate league. It should feel like the people who run it: people who love kids' soccer, show up on Saturdays in folding chairs, and don't take themselves too seriously. The AYSO national tone — *Everyone Plays*, focus on learning not winning — sits at the center of every design decision.

Voice (from CLAUDE.md): straightforward, factual, helpful, inclusive. No exclamation points. No editorializing ("Great news!"). No sarcasm. Minimal bolding. Plain language for tired parents reading at 9pm on a phone.

## Aesthetic Direction

**Light theme. Bold colors. Real photos. No stock.**

Locked-in elements (do not change):
- Colors: `#ff3c3c` light red, `#ce0e2d` dark red (logo red), `#a7ce57` green, `#f5bd4e` gold, `#8e2929` maroon, `#ede5d3` cream, `#231f20` dark
- Typography: Raleway (300–900) for everything
- Logos: existing AYSO Region 13 shield + AYSO/Section/Area affiliate marks

Design language:
- **Two-tone offset shapes** — buttons and tile labels have a solid body and a darker/lighter underline strip. This is the signature visual move — alternates direction across paired elements (e.g., red body + dark line, then dark body + red line).
- **Big bold uppercase display type** for hero/section headlines; sentence-case for body copy.
- **Cream surface, maroon accents, red primary** — the cream background grounds everything; maroon is for structure (action bars, frames); red is for emphasis (buttons, headlines, labels).
- **Photography is the visual centerpiece** — every program tile, hero, and gallery uses real Region 13 player photos. Never stock. Never clip art. Never illustrated mascots.

Anti-references:
- Cheesy or stock photography of any kind
- Generic "youth-sports-org" template aesthetics — clip-art soccer balls, swoosh graphics, "kid fonts"
- Glassmorphism, glow effects, gradient text, side-stripe accent borders
- Corporate league polish — this is a community org, not a franchise

## Page Hierarchy

**Home page is the magazine cover. Inner pages are utilitarian.**

- **Home**: loud, bold, photo-driven. The hero is huge. Tiles and overlays carry the two-tone treatment. This is where the brand personality is most expressed.
- **Inner pages** (programs, fields, parents, coaches, etc.): consistent with the home (same colors, same type, same buttons), but simpler. Prose-heavy. Sidebar nav. Scannable. **Easy to edit** — board members and a non-technical Laws-of-the-Game advisor maintain content via Pages CMS, so templates must keep complex layout out of editable content.

## Design Principles

1. **Real over polished.** Every photo is a real Region 13 kid on a real Region 13 field. If we don't have a photo for it, we use an existing photo or a solid color block — never stock, never AI-generated imagery, never clip art.
2. **Loud at the door, calm inside.** The home page does the heavy lifting on personality. Inner pages prioritize readability and editability over visual impact.
3. **Bold over busy.** Solid color blocks, sharp edges, two-tone offsets. No gradients on text, no glass effects, no decorative sparklines. Restraint in technique amplifies impact.
4. **Plain language for tired parents.** Content is written for someone on their phone between games, not for a copy editor. Short sentences, concrete instructions, no jargon, no hype words.
5. **Editor-friendly templates.** Anything board members or content advisors edit must use simple structured fields in Pages CMS, never raw HTML or layout-encoding markdown. Visual treatments live in templates, not in content.
