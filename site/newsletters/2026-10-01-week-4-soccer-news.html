We Did It!
Heading into last weekend, coaches, refs, players, and families asked themselves, “Games without voices from the sideline… can that actually happen?” It did! 

Bravo, Region 13—you made Silent Saturday a success. As one spectator shared, “The communication between the kids on the field was fun to watch.”

As we head deeper into the season, with teams checking the standings in competitive divisions and jockeying for position in the Rose City Cup, let’s carry forward the lessons from Silent Saturday to best support everyone out on the field.

This Weekend is Going to Be Hot
With another heat wave headed our way, coaches and referees will be checking the latest conditions at ayso13.org/temp and ensuring that games are adjusted accordingly to keep everyone safe. 

In accordance with our heat policy, AYSO Region 13 follows California Interscholastic Federation (CIF) heat acclimatization guidelines. We monitor Wet Bulb Globe Temperature (WBGT) and adjust or cancel activities based on the alert level:
What You Can Do...
  • Hydrate before, during, and after games. 
  • Watch for heat illness signs. Cramping, dizziness, headache, confusion, hot and dry skin, stopped sweating, nausea. If you see them, stop the activity, get the player into shade, cool them with water, and call for help. Heat stroke is a medical emergency.
  • Communicate—Coaches and referees can always shorten or end games at any time they feel conditions are unsafe to continue. If you notice players on the field struggling, tell a coach or ref during a break in play.

Letter from the Commissioner

Hello everyone,

Last weekend we held Region 13’s first-ever Silent Saturday. Across a dozen fields, we tried something new: quieting the adults and letting the youth run the show. Only the players could talk, and it was pretty amazing. I watched 10U kids on the field formulating a response to a free kick. I saw coaches bring a chair so that they could sit and watch their players. I observed parents with pom-poms and signs who found that cheering their kids on was easier and more fun than trying to protect them and tell them what to do. 

Although I know that some coaches felt stymied, while others thought it was amazing, I can say that every bit of feedback from our children was positive. I talked to one child after their game, and he said that it was the best game they had played. I think we all learned something about why we say, “Coaches coach, players play, and parents cheer”.

Let’s see if we can bring that feeling through the rest of the season.



—Patrick Shopbell, your volunteer RC
