Silent Saturday Ahead…
On September 26, Silent Saturday gives kids a chance to play a purer form of soccer, free from coaches' and spectators' direction from the sidelines. 

The goals of Silent Saturday are:
  • To emphasize that the game is about letting the kids have fun and play.
  • To show that kids can play well on their own with limited instruction.
  • To help the few parents and coaches who feel they must provide constant direction, understand how disruptive it can be.
  • To give players the chance to trust their skills and instincts without sideline input.
  • To encourage leadership skills among the individual players as they have the unique opportunity of giving their own instruction on the field.
  • To encourage a sense of true teamwork as the players must learn to rely upon one another and communicate with each other accordingly.
  • To support our volunteer referees, both youth and adult, by eliminating sideline interference and comments.
While coaches and family members are asked to remain silent… clapping is allowed! Be creative in how you choose to cheer your child’s team – make signs to hold up or bring a rally towel in the team’s color and wave it wildly. We’ll be hosting a poster-making station at Victory Park to get your arts and crafts on! There are lots of ways to cheer other than verbally – applaud/clap, but please no horns, whistles, or noise-makers.

Letter from the Commissioner

Hello everyone,
As you probably know by now, AYSO is guided by six philosophies. This week, I want us to think about one of these: Good Sportsmanship.

Good sportsmanship is critical for every single one of us to display at all times. Whether we are volunteer coaches or referees, players in the game, parents watching, or simply observers at the park or practice field. AYSO emphasizes Good Sportsmanship over winning in all of our games and activities. I firmly believe that is what keeps families and players coming back year after year. When you are on that field cheering on your team, remember that some of the parents and kids on the other team are probably going to be on your team next year. Supporting everyone helps all of us to enjoy the beautiful game.

And remember: our kids are watching everything we do. If they see us criticizing the coach or blaming a referee for what happens on that field, how do they incorporate that into their concept of practicing hard and playing as a team? Part of Good Sportsmanship is recognizing that everyone makes mistakes, and that is part of learning and growing—which is exactly what we are trying to do with our children every day.

Thank you to each of you for sharing that journey with AYSO and Region 13.


—Patrick Shopbell, your volunteer RC
