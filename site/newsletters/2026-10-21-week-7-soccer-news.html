Counting Down in Week 7
We’re past the halfway point and into the final month of the regular season. Teams in competitive divisions are jockeying for position and looking ahead to tournament play. It’s hard to believe that the end is in view, but there are lots of ways to keep playing past the Fall:

Winter Stars
This 8-week program offers skills training and small-sided games for players ages 6 and up on Sundays during the winter months. Designed to be casual and low-commitment, players are grouped by age and experience for small-sided scrimmage games. There are no permanent teams, and this program welcomes players of all skill levels.

Grad Series
Grad Series is for players transitioning from 8U to 10U. This program focuses on preparing for the changes that come in our competitive divisions, including larger fields, goalkeepers, and offside rules.

Beyond the Ball Coaching Corner
Who Haven't You Heard From?

As the season winds down, you probably know which players are quick to answer questions, volunteer for demonstrations, or share their opinions. But what about the quieter players? Some children need more time, a different invitation, or a little less attention on them before they're ready to contribute.

At Your Next Practice: 
  • NOTICE: Who regularly takes the lead? Who tends to hang back? Adjust: Create opportunities for different players to make decisions during play. Try: During a small-sided game, invite different players to suggest a new rule, demonstrate a skill, or identify an opportunity they see on the field. Rotate these opportunities so everyone has a chance to contribute.
Sensory Sideline: Flip the Script! 
Same players. Same field. Different game! With a few games left, surprise your team with five minutes of soccer handball. Players move the ball by throwing and catching instead of kicking. Keep everyone moving, encourage teamwork, and let the laughter happen!

Throwing and catching provide muscle-and-joint feedback (proprioception), helping players practice body awareness and control. Notice: Who comes alive when the activity changes? Does a different kind of game bring out participation you haven't seen lately? Sometimes flipping the script opens another door to learning.

Letter from the Commissioner

Hello everyone,

Soccer is impressive in its simplicity. All it really requires is a ball and something to mark a couple of goals. This probably explains its worldwide popularity. Of course, we add a few things in AYSO: equipment to make it safe (shin guards), volunteer coaches and referees to encourage fair play and the development of technique and sportsmanship, and painted fields that make it easier to conduct games.

You may not be aware, but soccer fields are possibly the most difficult aspect of our program, especially for our big fall season. Many, many sports vie for the same fields at the same time, and environmental issues put additional pressure on the fields. Those gophers at Victory Park are cute, but wow, they make a mess of the field. Protecting those limited fields is also the reason that we do not play in rainy conditions, which might damage the grass.

We owe a big thanks to the cities of Pasadena and La Cañada, as well as to the Pasadena Unified School District (PUSD). Without these entities granting us use of their fields, our soccer program would simply not exist. If you attend a school in one of our towns, please let your principal know how much we appreciate this support!

See you out on those fields this weekend.

Patrick Shopbell
Your volunteer Regional Commissioner
