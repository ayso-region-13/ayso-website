Abuse of referees has escalated across youth sports and is now among the top reasons game officials leave. Nearly every referee in Region 13 is a volunteer, and many are children. When they walk away, games do not get played.

AYSO adopted the U.S. Soccer Referee Abuse Prevention standards into national policy effective March 2025. Area 1C approved its implementation protocols in July 2026 and enforces them across all nine regions, including Region 13, beginning with the Fall 2026 season. The program is known as RAPP.

## What we expect from everyone

Referees ref, coaches coach, parents cheer. The call on the field stands.

Frustration over a call is understandable, and a muttered "ugh, she missed that one" is part of watching sport. What is not acceptable is dissent taken to a public, personal level: "you suck," "how much is that team paying you," "you're an idiot." There is also never a reason to touch a referee outside a handshake.

Coaches are responsible for the conduct of their players and their spectators. Under the U.S. Soccer policy, a referee is protected from the moment they arrive at the venue, including the parking lot, until they leave it, and that protection extends to family members and guests who came with them.

## What counts as referee abuse

RAPP is narrower than general misconduct. It covers language and behavior that is extreme and deliberate, and that causes significant harm or shows a material lack of respect. Ordinary dissent is handled on the field, as described in the next section.

Verbal abuse falls into four levels:

| Level | Behavior |
|---|---|
| 1 | Verbal taunting: insulting, belittling, insinuating, or taunting that undermines referee authority |
| 2 | Harassment or intimidation: retaliation, abusive or threatening language |
| 3 | Threats or derogatory language: aggression, attacking, cyberbullying, doxing, threats of violence |
| 4 | Offensive or discriminatory act: words or actions based on race, skin color, ethnicity, nationality, social origin, gender, disability, sexual orientation, language, or religion |

Physical abuse falls into three levels:

| Level | Behavior |
|---|---|
| 1 | Minor or slight deliberate touching: tapping a shoulder, touching the chest, bumping, pulling on a jersey |
| 2 | Intentional confrontation: pushing, grabbing, pulling, squeezing, pinching, slapping, using an object in a non-striking manner, damaging property |
| 3 | Violent contact: hitting, punching, elbowing, kicking, biting, spitting, choking, tackling, throwing or striking with an object |

Consequences are set by the U.S. Soccer Penalties Matrix, not by Region 13. Where abuse or assault is verified, the person is automatically suspended pending a hearing.

## Dissent: warn, caution, dismiss

Most sideline behavior never rises to a RAPP violation, and referees should keep using the tools the Laws of the Game already provide. Do not be shy about using them.

1. First instance, from a player, coach, or spectator: warn them that the comments will not be tolerated.
2. Next instance, for players and coaches: issue a Caution (yellow card). For spectators: ask the coach to intervene, with a clear message that the next instance means ejection from the field.
3. If it continues: Send-Off (red card) for the player or coach, ejection for the spectator.

## When to send off or eject immediately

Skip the ladder entirely and send off or eject on the spot if the behavior involves:

- Foul language or actions
- Racial, gender, ethnic, or religious slurs
- Abuse directed personally at you or at a player

## Report it

Region 13 cannot address this problem without knowing where and when it is happening. Report every incident, including ones you resolved on the field.

**During a match**, you may stop play and ask the coaches for help, including help identifying a spectator by name. Issue the caution, send-off, or ejection the situation calls for, then finish the match.

**After the match**, report it two ways:

1. Contact the Regional Referee Administrator at [referee@ayso13.org](mailto:referee@ayso13.org) right away. Do this the same day. The RRA notifies the Area Referee Administrator and makes a preliminary determination on whether the incident meets the RAPP standard.
2. File a written report using the [Referee Incident Form](https://ayso13.typeform.com/to/GjC4Pyed). Include what was said or done in specific terms, the name of the person responsible, and any match details that give context.

If the RRA or ARA confirms a RAPP violation, the report goes to the Area 1C RAPP Administrator, who investigates further and may refer it to the RAPP Disciplinary Panel. The panel, not Region 13, determines sanctions. The full sequence is in the [flow chart](/assets/docs/area-1c-rapp-flow-chart-2026.pdf).

Reports about a referee's *performance* rather than someone's conduct toward a referee go to the [Referee Feedback Form](https://ayso13.typeform.com/referee-fb) instead.

Coaches, players, spectators, and assistant referees can all report an incident. It does not have to come from the center referee.
